01 / The concept

A simple premise.
A real payoff.

Make the viewer understand the challenge instantly, then use real gameplay decisions and escalating encounters to hold attention.

◎

The mission

Queue solo against squads and attempt to defeat a full enemy squad. Keep the rules clear and the result honest.

↗

The tension

Show meaningful choices, risky fights, mistakes, and close calls. Remove long stretches of looting and empty travel.

✦

The payoff

End with the decisive fight or the closest attempt. A loss can still make a satisfying story if the challenge is delivered.

Success condition: The footage must show the real result. Don't promise a squad wipe if you didn't get one. Viewers are irritatingly good at noticing.
02 / Retention map

The 8–10 minute
video structure.

Use these timestamps as a rough edit plan. Let the quality of the footage decide the final length.

00:00–00:15

Cold open

Start with a real intense moment. State the challenge immediately.

00:15–01:00

Set the rules

Explain solo vs squad and what counts as success.

01:00–03:00

Build tension

Show setup, decisions, footsteps, and first contact. Cut dead time.

03:00–06:00

Escalate

Show the key fight, mistake, adaptation, or tactical shift.

06:00–09:30

Payoff

Deliver the final encounter, then give a short verdict and next challenge tease.

◉

Pattern interrupt

Use a short replay, freeze-frame, zoom, or quick voice-over when it adds context. Don't throw random effects at the screen.

♫

Audio hierarchy

Keep footsteps and gunfire audible. Voice-over should be clear and music should sit quietly underneath.

↳

End-screen bridge

Point to a related challenge video once you have one. Avoid a long subscription speech after the story is already over.
